# Style guide distilled from the reference letters

- Address the named company's hiring team when the company is known; otherwise use “Dear Hiring Team”.
- Name the exact role in the opening and connect one distinctive part of the job description to the candidate's motivation.
- Build the middle around two to four requirements that have strong evidence in the candidate profile.
- Prefer concrete work and outcomes over adjective-heavy self-description.
- Explain transferable experience honestly when direct domain experience is absent.
- Connect technical work to product, customer, operational, teaching, or business impact as appropriate.
- Keep the tone confident, warm, and professional. Avoid flattery, clichés, and exaggerated claims.
- Use short prose paragraphs by default. Bullets are acceptable only when they materially improve a requirements-heavy letter.
- Close with a specific contribution the candidate could make, then a brief thank-you and signature.
- Aim for roughly 300–450 words unless the caller requests another length.

## Grounding rules

- Candidate facts come only from the selected profile.
- Company and role facts come only from the supplied job description and explicit CLI arguments.
- Never infer a metric, date, credential, employer, tool, responsibility, language level, location preference, or industry background.
- Do not silently turn adjacent experience into direct experience.
- Omit unsupported requirements; if a gap is important, frame verified transferable experience without pretending the gap does not exist.
